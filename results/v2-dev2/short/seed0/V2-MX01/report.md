# Findings report: Elizabeth Holmes was convicted of defrauding both investors and patients.

## Bottom line
**FALSE** — unlikely (25%), as of 2026-10-01.  
**Subject type:** claim

The claim that Elizabeth Holmes was convicted of defrauding both investors and patients is FALSE. She was convicted of investor fraud (one conspiracy count and three wire fraud counts) in January 2022, but was acquitted of all patient‑fraud charges and an additional patient‑related wire fraud count was dismissed at the same time. Her sentence was later reduced from 135 to 123 months in March 2026, and her conviction and sentence were upheld by the Ninth Circuit in February 2025.

## Key findings
- Elizabeth Holmes was convicted of investor fraud: one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud and three counts of wire fraud involving investments over $140 million. [1D][2D][3D][4D][5C][6D] _(credibility 1: confirmed)_
- Elizabeth Holmes was acquitted of patient fraud charges: she was acquitted of a patient-related conspiracy to commit wire fraud and three other wire fraud counts, and an additional patient-related wire fraud charge was dismissed during trial. [1D][7B] _(credibility 2: probably true)_
- Her original 135‑month sentence was reduced to 123 months in March 2026 after a federal judge granted relief under a retroactive sentencing guidelines amendment. [1D][8D][9D] _(credibility 1: confirmed)_
- The Ninth Circuit upheld Holmes’s conviction, sentence, and restitution order in February 2025, rejecting her appeal. [10D][11C][12D][13C] _(credibility 3: possibly true)_
- Walgreens settled claims over fraudulent Theranos tests, offering patients double their payment if the settlement is approved. [14D] _(credibility 6: cannot be judged)_
