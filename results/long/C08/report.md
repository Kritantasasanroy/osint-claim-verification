# Findings report: The Great Wall of China is visible to the naked eye from the Moon.

**Subject type:** claim  
**Verdict:** FALSE  
**Confidence:** 92%

## Summary
The claim that the Great Wall of China is visible to the naked eye from the Moon is false. The Wall is only 4–5 m wide, which at the average Earth‑Moon distance of ~384,400 km subtends an angle of about 0.002 arcseconds—far below the human eye's resolution limit of roughly 60 arcseconds under ideal conditions. Consequently, the Wall cannot be discerned without aid, as confirmed by authoritative sources.

## Key findings
- The Great Wall of China has a typical width of about 4–5 meters at its top. [1][2][3][4] _(confidence 95%)_
- The average distance from Earth to the Moon is approximately 384,400 kilometers. [5][6] _(confidence 96%)_
- At the Moon's distance, the Great Wall's width subtends an angle of roughly 0.002 arcseconds. [7] _(confidence 90%)_
- Because the Wall's angular size (~0.002 arcsec) is far below the eye's resolution limit (~60 arcsec), it cannot be seen with the naked eye from the Moon. [7][1][5] _(confidence 92%)_

## Conflicting information
- No open conflict between independent sources.

## Gaps and limitations
- No direct source was found for the human eye's angular resolution limit under optimal conditions; this value is taken from general knowledge.
- The Wall's angular size from the Moon was derived indirectly via analogy (human hair at 3 km) rather than a direct measurement.
