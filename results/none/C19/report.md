# Findings report: Drinking red wine in moderation is good for heart health.

**Subject type:** claim  
**Verdict:** UNVERIFIABLE  
**Confidence:** 85%

## Summary
Current evidence does not substantiate the claim that moderate red wine consumption is good for heart health. There is a lack of high‑quality randomized controlled trials showing reduced cardiovascular disease or mortality, observational findings are confounded by lifestyle factors, and major health authorities do not recommend alcohol for cardiovascular protection while noting its cancer and addiction risks. Consequently, the claim remains unverifiable with high confidence.

## Key findings
- No high-quality randomized controlled trials demonstrate a statistically significant reduction in cardiovascular disease incidence or mortality from moderate red wine consumption compared to abstinence. [1] _(confidence 70%)_
- Observational associations between moderate red wine intake and better heart health are heavily confounded by lifestyle factors such as diet, exercise, and socioeconomic status. [1] _(confidence 65%)_
- Major health authorities (American Heart Association, World Health Organization, European Society of Cardiology) do not endorse moderate red wine consumption as a means to improve cardiovascular health and emphasize its risks. [1] _(confidence 80%)_
- Moderate alcohol consumption carries documented risks, including increased cancer risk, liver disease, and addiction potential, which may offset any putative cardiovascular benefits. [1] _(confidence 60%)_
- There are no large-scale, long-term randomized controlled trials specifically testing moderate red wine intake versus abstinence on hard cardiovascular endpoints such as myocardial infarction, stroke, or cardiovascular mortality. [1] _(confidence 75%)_

## Conflicting information
- Are there any high-quality observational studies that, after adjusting for confounding factors, demonstrate a causal link between moderate red wine intake and improved heart health markers (e.g., LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, blood pressure, endothelial function)? - supported by acc.org, mayoclinic.org, mdpi.com, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, ucdavis.edu (weight 2.58) vs refuted by healthline.com, pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, theheartfoundation.org (weight 1.75)

## Gaps and limitations
- Absence of long-term randomized controlled trials with clinical cardiovascular endpoints for red wine versus abstinence.
- Inability of observational studies to fully control for confounding lifestyle and socioeconomic factors.
- Limited and heterogeneous data on cancer risk from wine consumption, preventing precise risk‑benefit quantification.
- Lack of head‑to‑head trials comparing red wine to other alcoholic beverages or to non‑alcoholic polyphenol sources.
- Insufficient quantitative risk‑benefit analyses that use comparable metrics (e.g., QALYs) for cardiovascular benefits versus cancer, liver disease, and addiction harms.
