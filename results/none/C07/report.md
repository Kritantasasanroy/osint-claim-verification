# Findings report: Elizabeth Holmes was acquitted of all charges in her 2022 fraud trial.

**Subject type:** claim  
**Verdict:** FALSE  
**Confidence:** 92%

## Summary
Elizabeth Holmes was not acquitted of all charges in her 2022 fraud trial. The trial concluded on January 3, 2022 with a partial verdict: guilty on four counts of defrauding investors, not guilty on four counts of defrauding patients, and a mistrial on three investor fraud counts. She was later sentenced on November 18, 2022 to 135 months in prison. The claim that she was acquitted of all charges is therefore false.

## Key findings
- Elizabeth Holmes's fraud trial concluded on January 3, 2022 with a partial verdict. [1][2][3] _(confidence 93%)_
- The jury found Holmes guilty on four counts of defrauding investors (three wire fraud, one conspiracy), not guilty on four counts of defrauding patients, and unable to reach a unanimous verdict on three counts of wire fraud (mistrial). [1][2][3] _(confidence 92%)_
- The charges brought against Holmes were defrauding investors (wire fraud and conspiracy) and defrauding patients. [4][3] _(confidence 94%)_
- Holmes was not acquitted of all charges; she was convicted on four investor fraud counts. [4][5] _(confidence 91%)_
- The official source documenting the verdict and sentencing is the U.S. Department of Justice page for the Northern District of California case (justice.gov/usao-ndca/us-v-elizabeth-holmes-et-al). [4] _(confidence 95%)_

## Conflicting information
- No open conflict between independent sources.
  - Assessment: Some Wikipedia edits state that on July 7, 2022 the jury returned a verdict of guilty on all 12 counts, which contradicts multiple independent sources (DOJ, NYT, BBC, ABC News) that record a partial verdict on January 3, 2022 with guilty on four investor fraud counts, not guilty on four patient fraud counts, and a mistrial on three investor fraud counts. The July 7 claim was given low weight due to lack of corroboration and likely editorial error, while the January 3 partial verdict was supported by high‑weight sources (DOJ weight 0.95, NYT weight 0.85, BBC weight 0.85) and therefore accepted as the accurate record.
