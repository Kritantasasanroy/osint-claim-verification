# Findings report: The Great Wall of China is visible to the naked eye from the Moon.

**Subject type:** claim  
**Verdict:** FALSE  
**Confidence:** 90%

## Summary
The Great Wall of China is only a few meters wide, while the Moon is about 384,000 km away, giving the wall an angular size far below the resolution of the human eye. Consequently, it cannot be seen with the naked eye from the Moon, a conclusion supported by authoritative sources. Evidence gaps remain regarding exact human eye resolution, atmospheric scattering details, and low‑Earth‑orbit visibility claims.

## Key findings
- The Great Wall of China's typical width at the top ranges from 4 to 5 meters. [1][2][3][4] _(confidence 90%)_
- The average distance from Earth to the Moon is about 384,400 kilometers. [5][6][7][8][9][10][11] _(confidence 95%)_
- As seen from the Moon, the Great Wall's angular diameter is extremely small—comparable to that of a human hair viewed from 3 km away. [9] _(confidence 70%)_
- The Great Wall of China cannot be seen by the naked human eye from the Moon. [9] _(confidence 90%)_

## Conflicting information
- No open conflict between independent sources.

## Gaps and limitations
- Precise angular resolution of the human eye under optimal conditions (in arcseconds) is not provided in the evidence.
- Quantitative assessment of atmospheric scattering effects on visibility of terrestrial objects from the Moon is lacking.
- Specific, verifiable claims of Great Wall visibility from low Earth orbit, including viewing conditions and instrumentation, are absent from the evidence.
