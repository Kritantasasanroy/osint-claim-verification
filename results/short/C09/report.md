# Findings report: Theranos's Edison device could reliably run hundreds of blood tests from a single finger-prick of blood.

**Subject type:** claim  
**Verdict:** FALSE  
**Confidence:** 85%

## Summary
The claim that Theranos's Edison device could reliably run hundreds of blood tests from a single finger‑prick sample is false; evidence shows the device lacked independent validation, received FDA clearance for only one test, and was halted for all but one test due to reliability issues.

## Key findings
- Theranos publicly claimed the Edison device could run hundreds of blood tests from a single finger‑prick sample with accuracy comparable to traditional methods. [1][2][3] _(confidence 90%)_
- Independent validation or regulatory assessment showed only one Theranos test (HSV‑1 IgG) received FDA clearance; no evidence the Edison could reliably perform hundreds of distinct tests from finger‑prick blood. [4][3][5] _(confidence 90%)_
- The 2015 Wall Street Journal investigation and subsequent FDA/CMS inspections found Theranos halted finger‑prick blood collection for all but one test, indicating reliability problems with the Edison device. [6][3][7] _(confidence 85%)_
- Theranos never obtained FDA clearance, approval, or formal authorization for the Edison device to perform the claimed hundreds of tests from a finger‑prick sample; only the HSV‑1 test was cleared. [4][3][5] _(confidence 90%)_

## Conflicting information
- No open conflict between independent sources.

## Gaps and limitations
- No peer‑reviewed validation studies demonstrating the Edison’s ability to run hundreds of tests from finger‑prick blood.
- Limited publicly available internal test data from the 2018 criminal trial detailing error rates for individual assays on the Edison.
- Absence of a post‑mortem hardware analysis explaining why the Edison could not scale to hundreds of assays.
